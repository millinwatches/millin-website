MASTER PROMPT FOR FIGMA
HOUSE OF MILLIN
DIGITAL MAISON EXPERIENCE
MASTER CREATIVE DIRECTION PROMPT

Redesign and evolve the existing MILLIN digital experience into a complete Digital Maison Experience.

This is NOT an e-commerce website.
This is NOT a traditional luxury watch website.
This is NOT a product catalogue.
This is NOT a visual redesign based primarily on watches.

The website must feel like entering a private international Maison dedicated to horological art.

The visitor should feel that they have entered:

a private salon,
an atelier,
a library,
an archive,
a cultural institution,
and finally a world of exceptional mechanical creations.

The website should unfold like a film.

Like walking through a private residence.

Like turning the pages of a rare art book.

Like being invited into the world of a House that exists for a small circle of collectors.

The fundamental principle is:

HERO SPACE
→ HERO CRAFT
→ HERO HUMAN
→ HERO CREATION
→ HERO COLLECTOR
→ HERO LEGACY

The watch is the culmination of the story.

Never the beginning.

--------------------------------------------------
01 — BRAND POSITIONING
--------------------------------------------------

Position House of MILLIN as:

A House of Horological Art.

A contemporary Maison preserving rare Métiers d'Art and transforming them into exceptional mechanical creations.

The brand must feel closer to:

a private European Maison,
an art institution,
a collector's residence,
a historic atelier,
a contemporary museum,

than to:

a watch retailer,
an e-commerce store,
a jewellery boutique,
a fashion brand,
or a conventional luxury watch manufacturer.

The core positioning:

HOUSE OF MILLIN
A House of Horological Art.

Alternative editorial statement:

Handcrafted horological art,
created for a handful of collectors.

The brand should communicate:

patience,
craftsmanship,
rarity,
culture,
human mastery,
hospitality,
discretion,
and permanence.

Never communicate:

mass luxury,
commercial urgency,
discounts,
sales pressure,
product obsession,
or aggressive conversion.

--------------------------------------------------
02 — THE PRIMARY EXPERIENCE
--------------------------------------------------

The website must be experienced as a journey through the House.

The visitor should move through spaces rather than website sections.

The digital architecture should feel like:

Entrance Hall
→ Private Salon
→ Library
→ Atelier
→ Métiers d'Art
→ Gallery
→ Bespoke Atelier
→ Collector Experience
→ Boutique
→ Archive / Journal
→ Legacy

The scroll should feel like walking through rooms.

Every transition should feel like passing through a doorway.

Every new chapter should reveal a different emotional state.

Do not use conventional section separators.

Use:

light transitions,
architectural transitions,
darkness,
whitespace,
slow image reveals,
full-screen imagery,
video,
material changes,
and subtle motion.

Large typography.

Large negative space.

Show:

masters,
designers,
collectors,

No exaggerated smiles.

No fashion campaign styling.

People should:

read,

11 — LIGHT CONSTITUTION
--------------------------------------------------

Light is the first architect of the House.

Use:

natural daylight,
soft window light,
museum lighting,
warm reflected light,
fire,
and gentle shadow.

The room receives light before the object.

Preferred lighting:

morning daylight,
overcast daylight,
late afternoon,
soft museum evening.

Avoid:

neon,
RGB lighting,
hard flash,
fashion lighting,
aggressive rim light,
overexposure,
and artificial luxury effects.

The House has four emotional light states:

ARRIVAL LIGHT
Soft morning light.

CRAFT LIGHT
Honest atelier light.

COLLECTOR LIGHT
Warm private salon light.

LEGACY LIGHT
Quiet museum/archive light.

Use lighting to communicate where the visitor is emotionally.

--------------------------------------------------
12 — COMPOSITION SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------

Composition must feel architectural.

Use:

generous negative space,
calm geometry,
deep perspective,
layered foreground,
middle ground,
background,
and controlled asymmetry.

The composition hierarchy:

SPACE
→ LIGHT
→ GEOMETRY
→ HUMAN
→ CRAFT
→ CREATION
→ DETAIL

One hero per frame.

Never overcrowd.

Use the 70/30 principle:

70% atmosphere and space.
30% subject.

Negative space is a defining luxury signal.

The frame should feel discovered,
not staged.

Use partial revelation.

Do not show everything.

Allow the House to continue beyond the frame.

Preferred camera language:

24mm — architecture
35mm — interiors
50mm — editorial storytelling
85mm — portraits
100mm macro — craftsmanship
135mm — compression

Preferred camera height:

145–160cm for architectural photography.

The camera should behave like a respectful guest.

--------------------------------------------------
13 — HOROLOGICAL PHOTOGRAPHY
--------------------------------------------------

Only after the visitor understands the House should the creations become central.

Treat watches as museum objects.

Never as catalogue products.

Every creation should be documented through:

1. Portrait of Place
2. Portrait of Form
3. Portrait of Light
4. Portrait of Craft
5. Portrait of Time
6. Portrait of Belonging
7. Portrait of Legacy

The watch should appear:

inside architecture,
inside a library,
on an atelier table,
beside sketches,
in the hands of an artisan,
in the hands of a collector,
or inside the House archive.

Never make the watch float in empty space.

Never use excessive reflections.

Never use commercial product lighting.

Never use aggressive effects.

The watch should glow.

It should never sparkle theatrically.

31 — FINAL CREATIVE TEST
--------------------------------------------------

Before approving any screen, ask:

Does the House come before the product?

Does Space come before Creation?

Does Craft come before Commerce?

Does Human Presence create emotional credibility?

Does the visual feel timeless?

Does it feel inhabited?

Does it feel culturally meaningful?

Is there enough silence?

Is there enough negative space?

Would this belong in the House Bible?

Would a collector want to spend time here?

Would the experience still feel relevant in twenty years?

If not,
redesign.
